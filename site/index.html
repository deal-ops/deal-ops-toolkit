How it works

Step 1 · Reconciliation

Every receipt matched, or explained

Import the investor list and the bank statement. A receipt is booked automatically only when its reference contains exactly one investor's payment reference; everything else is a suggestion with a score and reasons, and a person confirms it.

In the app: three suggested matches confirmed, and the summary updates.

Step 2 · Closing allocation

Know what everyone invests before the wire

Fees, scale-back and over-payment refunds for every investor, with a closing confirmation you can copy and check.

In the app: the closing totals, then each investor's line.

Step 3 · Exit distribution

Carry, hurdle and catch-up, shown step by step

Split exit proceeds between investors and the lead, and see how each pound got where it went.

In the app: exit proceeds changed from £250,000 to £390,000; the carry to the lead and the investors' share update.

FAQ

Is it for me?

Who is it for?

Syndicate leads and deal operations teams running an SPV round, and angels who want to see how an exit is split. No data of your own yet? Load the sample deal and take the 3-minute tour.

What does it not do?

It does not move money, connect to a bank or send emails. It does not produce legal documents, run KYC or AML checks, or file anything with a regulator or tax authority. It does not decide S/EIS eligibility, and it is not tax, legal or investment advice. It does not convert currencies. The exit split covers one investment and one exit with the same terms for every investor: no staged capital calls, earlier distributions, management fees, VAT or individual carry terms.

What deal structure does it assume?

An SPV that holds the shares through a nominee (bare trust): the nominee is on the company’s share register and investors hold beneficial interests, so fractional shares are allowed. Investors holding shares in the company directly are not supported.

Do I need an account?

No. It runs in your browser: no sign-up, nothing to install. It is a working prototype, and the code is open source under the Apache-2.0 licence.

Your data

Where does my deal data go?

Your deal data is processed in your browser and never sent by this app. The hosting provider still receives normal page requests. It is saved in this browser so you can come back to it, and “Clear all data” removes it. Deal data never appears in the page address, and there are no analytics.

Can I import my own investor list and bank statement?

Yes, as CSV: choose a file or paste the text. The investor list needs a name, a payment reference and the cash due; the bank statement needs a date and an amount, plus the payer and reference for matching. Common header names are accepted, such as “paid in” or “credit” for the amount and “narrative” or “description” for the reference. Payments out are skipped and listed. If any row has an error, nothing is imported and every error is listed with its row and column. Example files are in the GitHub repository.

Which currencies?

One currency per deal: GBP, EUR or USD. Amounts are never converted; changing the currency only relabels them, and the app asks first. While a fee payer change is active, the currency is locked until you reverse it.

What can I take away?

CSV exports of the reconciliation ledger, the chase list, the allocation, the holdings, the exit distribution and the activity log, each with a currency column. The closing confirmation can be copied as text.

Common situations

A payment has no reference, or came from a company or family office account.

A receipt is booked automatically only when its reference contains exactly one investor’s payment reference. Anything else gets up to three suggested investors with a score and the reasons, and stays unbooked until a person confirms it, assigns it to someone else or marks it as not part of this deal. “Copy query” gives you a short note to ask the investor; it mentions only the date, the amount and the currency. Receipts left unmatched for more than 10 days (you can change the number) are flagged for review.

An investor says they paid, but nothing has arrived.

The chase list shows who is short or has not paid, and by how much. “Copy message” gives you an email to send yourself; it asks anyone who has already paid to get the MT103 (or equivalent) showing the UETR from their bank, so the payment can be traced. The app never sends anything.

The round is oversubscribed, or someone is below the minimum.

Over the cap, everyone is scaled back pro rata and the excess is listed as refunds. Investors below the minimum commitment are refunded in full. If scale-back takes someone below the minimum, the allocation says it cannot close and names them, so you can decide.

An investor withdraws before closing.

Mark them withdrawn with their written consent and a date; it can be undone. They are not chased and not carried into the allocation, everything they paid is listed to refund, and the closing confirmation lists them separately.

Who pays the fees, and can that change?

Investors (a percentage, taken from the payment or added on top, or a fixed amount each), the company (taken from the wire) or the lead (invoiced). Before closing you can move a percentage fee to a different payer: the app previews each investor’s top-up or refund and the consent needed, and the change can be reversed in one step. It needs the allocation carried in from reconciliation, up to date and not edited since; the preview blocks a change while the round is over its cap, or one that would take an investor below the minimum.

What carry terms are supported?

Fixed carry with an optional hurdle (compounded yearly) and catch-up, or tiered carry by return multiple, each tier at its own rate. Exit costs come off first, then capital is returned, then profit is split. Each step shows what went to investors and to the lead, and why.

Trust and status

How do I know the numbers are right?

Money is kept in whole pence or cents and the maths uses whole numbers only, with no floating point. A separate implementation, written in Python from the rules alone, is checked against the app on generated cases, and the tests are checked by deliberately breaking the code. Every calculation checks its own totals and shows no figures if a check fails. Still, check every result yourself before you rely on it.

Is it ready for real deals? How do I report a problem?

It is a working prototype: only what is built, tested and released is described here. Report problems by email, but please don’t send real investor details.
